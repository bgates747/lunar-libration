## 4. The effective torque is not a derived material transmission

**Analyst comparison with ordinary fixed-axis rigid-body dynamics:**

**τ_C,total = Iθ̈ = I(ψ̈ + β̈),**

**Iψ̈ = τ_C,total − Iβ̈.**

Comparing this with S2 gives **τ_C,total = 0 at every instant**. The equation
does not supply two independently identified material torques. Nor is it
merely a statement that a nonzero resultant torque averages to zero over a
cycle. Pointwise cancellation, zero integrated torque and zero net relative
turns are different statements; Appendix A7 keeps them separate.

**Source effective-torque formula — S1, section 5:**

**τ_inertial = −Iα.**

S1 passage quoted and explicitly endorsed by DREMT, [September 27, 2026 at 10:41 AM](https://www.drroyspencer.com/2026/09/uah-v6-1-global-temperature-update-for-august-2026-0-68-deg-c/#comment-1755435):

> This inertial torque physically drives the relative back-and-forth pendulum motion exactly as Tesla described.

DREMT, excerpt from the posted mechanical explanation, [September 28, 2026 at 5:40 AM](https://www.drroyspencer.com/2026/09/uah-v6-1-global-temperature-update-for-august-2026-0-68-deg-c/#comment-1755462):

> These torques would cause the moon to physically rock back and forth on its attachment point like a giant pendulum in space.

The −Iα term is the Euler contribution associated with changing angular rate
of the reporting axes. In the planar case, writing a mass element’s central
position as ρ = (x,y,z) and the frame acceleration as α_b ẑ gives

**dF_E = −dm α_b ẑ × ρ; τ_E,z = −α_b∫(x² + y²)dm = −I_zα_b.**

This follows from the rotating-coordinate acceleration transformation [R2].
It does not require a mass asymmetry. A uniform origin-translation inertial
field has zero central moment because ∫ρ dm = 0.

The September 28 explanation names centrifugal and d’Alembert forces across
the asymmetric mass distribution. For the planar centrifugal field,

**dF_cf = dm Ω²(x,y,0),**

**τ_cf,z = Ω²∫(xy − yx)dm = 0.**

That zero holds even for asymmetric density. It is a statement about the
central **z component**; it does not claim that all 3D centrifugal moments or
centrifugal stresses vanish. In this planar setting the source’s −Iα is the
Euler coordinate term, not a calculated centrifugal bulge torque.

**L_orb = μr²Ω = constant; μ = M_Em/(M_E + m).**

**H = L_orb + L_C**, not merely IΩ + Iω_s. A full multibody account has further
body and orbital terms. Supplying a Kepler orbit separately can consistently

## 8. Ideal monthly rocking and actual lunar apses

For an **analyst-specified fixed Kepler ellipse**, set perigee at t₀ = 0,
θ₀ = β₀ = 0 and C = n = 2π/T. With mean anomaly M = nt and true anomaly f,

**ψ = M − f; ψ ≈ −2e sin M to first order in eccentricity.**

The exact ideal waveform closes after one orbit, vanishes at perigee and
apogee, and has opposite equal extrema. At illustrative e = 0.0549, its
amplitude is approximately ±6.29325°. This is geometric evaluation of the
initialized rate balance, not an oscillator or restoring law supplied by
DREMT. See [the ideal-orbit implementation](src/keplerian_comparison.py).

For arbitrary supplied β(t), closure over an interval requires
**CΔt = β(t₂) − β(t₁)**. Bounded relative rocking does not imply exact closure
at arbitrary annual endpoints. Nor does the ideal illustration establish
exact alignment at actual lunar apses. The real orbit is perturbed, with
distinct sidereal and anomalistic periods [R3, printed pp. 2 and 5]. Apsidal precession
alone is not a general impossibility theorem: an ideal uniformly precessing
case can still admit closed relative motion. The missing source result is a
specified real-orbit/phase prediction, not proof that no such phase evolution
can ever be modeled. Exact apsis alignment in an animation is an analyst
condition, not a demonstrated source prediction.

**Information needed:** specify the orbital input or dynamics and whether the
claim is bounded rocking, endpoint closure or alignment at actual apses. Give
the phase/rate rule and test interval for the chosen claim
([I3](#i3-orbit-phase-and-closure-claim),
